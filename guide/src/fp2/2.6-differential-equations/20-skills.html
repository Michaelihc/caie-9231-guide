Skills

S1
Separate variables

When: Question has $\frac{dy}{dx}=$ (only-$x$ stuff) × (only-$y$ stuff), e.g. $\frac{dy}{dx}=xe^{-y}$. Or a substitution turned it into that.

  1. Get all $y$ with $dy$ on left, all $x$ with $dx$ on right.
  2. Integrate both sides. One $+C$, on the right.
  3. Initial condition ⇒ $C$. Rearrange for $y$ if asked.
Comes after a substitution

$y=ux$ (F5) or $z=x+y$ ⇒ separable in new variable. Sub back at end.

Can't make $y$ subject

Leave answer implicit (e.g. $a\ln(x+y)+b(x-y)+c=0$) unless Q asks for $y=$.

S2
1st order linear: integrating factor

When: 1st order: only $\frac{dy}{dx}$, no $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}$. $y$ and $\frac{dy}{dx}$ appear on their own, not squared, not multiplied together. Shape: (something)$\frac{dy}{dx}$ + (something)$\,y$ = (something), e.g. $x\frac{dy}{dx}+2y=e^x$.

  1. Divide every term by coefficient of $\frac{dy}{dx}$ ⇒ $\frac{dy}{dx}+P\,y=Q$. $P$ = what multiplies $y$ now, $Q$ = right side now.
  2. IF $=e^{\int P\,dx}$. Simplify. No $+C$.
  3. Work out IF × $Q$ (simplify). Write $\frac{d}{dx}(\text{IF}\cdot y)=\text{IF}\cdot Q$.
  4. Integrate right side ⇒ IF × $y$ = that $+\,C$. Add $+C$ now.
  5. Initial condition ⇒ $C$.
  6. Divide whole thing by IF. Answer must start $y=\dots$
Dividing first: $x\frac{dy}{dx}$, $\sin\theta\frac{dy}{dx}$, $(x^2+1)\frac{dy}{dx}$

Divide every term, right side too. $P$ is coefficient of $y$ after dividing.

Simplifying $e^{\int P}$
  • $e^{k\ln f}=f^k$: $\frac2x\to x^2$, $-\frac1x\to\frac1x$, $\frac{f'}{f}\to f$, $\frac{f'}{2f}\to\sqrt f$.
  • Trig/hyp: $\cot\theta\to\sin\theta$, $\tanh x\to\cosh x$, $2\tanh x\to\cosh^2x$, $\coth x\to\sinh x$.
  • Partial fractions first ⇒ IF is a ratio, e.g. $\frac{x}{x+7}$.
  • $\frac1{\sqrt{x^2+1}}\to e^{\sinh^{-1}x}=x+\sqrt{x^2+1}$ (log form of $\sinh^{-1}$).
  • Any non-zero multiple of IF works.
Right side hard to integrate
  • Part (a) shows $\frac{d}{dx}F=f$ ⇒ $\int f\,dx=F$. Reuse it, add $+C$.
  • $x\times e^{x}$ shape: by parts, $u=x$.
  • $e^{ax}\sin bx$: by parts twice ⇒ same integral comes back ⇒ move to left, divide.
  • Fraction: partial fractions, or $\frac{f'}{f}\to\ln f$. Root of quadratic: MF19 $\sinh^{-1}$/$\cosh^{-1}$ rows.
Part (a): "show that $\frac{d}{dx}(\dots)=\dots$"

Differentiate each term (inverse hyperbolic derivatives: MF19 Further Pure list). Common denominator. Simplify to the shown result.

Forgot $+C$ before dividing

Wrong: constant must end up as $\frac{C}{\text{IF}}$, not $C$.

Constant coefficient, e.g. $\frac{dy}{dx}+5y=e^{-7x}$

Still IF ($e^{5x}$). Same steps.

S3
2nd order constant coefficient: CF + PIuses F1 F2 F3

When: 2nd order with plain-number coefficients: $a\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}+b\frac{dy}{dx}+cy=$ (function of $x$), e.g. $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}+4\frac{dy}{dx}+5y=10\sin x$.

  1. Auxiliary equation ⇒ roots ⇒ CF.
  2. Trial PI from right side. Check it is not in CF.
  3. Differentiate trial twice. Sub trial and its derivatives into left side.
  4. Compare coefficients ⇒ unknowns.
  5. Write $y=$ CF + PI (in question's variable: $x$ or $t$).
Leading coefficient not 1, e.g. $9y''+6y'+y$

Keep it: $9m^2+6m+1=0$. Roots often fractions ($-\frac13$ repeated).

Right side only $\sin$ or only $\cos$

Trial needs both: $\lambda\cos px+\mu\sin px$.

Right side $27x^2$ only

Trial needs full $\lambda x^2+\mu x+\nu$. Compare $x^2$, $x$, constant terms.

Trial in CF: form given, e.g. $kxe^{-x}$ or $kx^2e^{2x}$

Differentiate given form with product rule. Sub. Solve for $k$. ($x^2$ when root is repeated.)

Disguised exponential on right side

$\cosh x+\sinh x=e^x$ ⇒ $(\cosh x+\sinh x)^{\frac12}=e^{\frac x2}$. Trial $\lambda e^{\frac x2}$.

Variable is $t$, e.g. $x(t)$ with $\frac{d^2x}{dt^2}$

Same steps. Read every $y$ as $x$ and every $x$ as $t$ in F1–F3. Answer starts $x=\dots$

Complex roots

Answer in real form $e^{px}(A\cos qx+B\sin qx)$. No $i$ in final answer.

S4
Particular solution from initial conditionsuses F1

When: Question gives values, e.g. "$y=1$ and $\frac{dy}{dx}=0$ when $x=0$", and asks for "the particular solution" or "the solution for which …".

  1. Start from full general solution (CF + PI). Keep both $A$, $B$.
  2. Sub $x=0$, $y=$ given ⇒ equation 1.
  3. Differentiate general solution. Sub $x=0$, $\frac{dy}{dx}=$ given ⇒ equation 2.
  4. Solve simultaneously. Write $y=\dots$ with numbers.
Repeated or complex root CF

Differentiating needs product rule: $\left((A+Bx)e^{\alpha x}\right)'=Be^{\alpha x}+\alpha(A+Bx)e^{\alpha x}$.

After a substitution

Convert conditions to new variable first. E.g. $v=y^4$: $v(0)=y(0)^4$, $v'=4y^3y'$. $y=\cosh u$: $y'=\sinh u\cdot u'$.

1st order

One condition, any point (e.g. $x=1$, $\theta=\frac\pi2$). Find $C$ before dividing by IF (S2).

S5
Given substitution ⇒ "show that equation becomes …"uses F4 F5

When: Question hands you a substitution ($v=y^4$, $x=e^t$, $y=ux$, …) and says "show that the equation becomes …".

  1. Write substitution. Differentiate it with respect to $x$: chain rule on $y$ terms, product rule on products.
  2. 2nd order target ⇒ differentiate again.
  3. Start from the shown equation's left side. Replace new variable and its derivatives by their old-variable forms. Simplify ⇒ it becomes (a multiple of) the original equation's left side ⇒ swap in original right side ⇒ matches.
  4. Solve new equation (S1, S2 or S3).
  5. Sub back ⇒ answer in original variables, e.g. $v=y^4$ ⇒ $y=v^{\frac14}$, $x=e^t$ ⇒ $t=\ln x$.
New dependent variable: $v=y^n$, $v=\frac1y$, $y=x^2w$, $y=\cosh u$, $z=xy$
  • $v=y^n$: $v'=ny^{n-1}y'$. Again (product rule): $v''=ny^{n-1}y''+n(n-1)y^{n-2}(y')^2$.
  • $y=x^2w$: $y'=2xw+x^2w'$, then product rule again.
  • Final answer: $y=\dots$ ($\cosh u=\dots$ also accepted).
New independent variable: $x=e^t$, $x=t^2$, $x=\frac1t$

$\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{dy/dt}{dx/dt}$. $x=e^t$: use F4. Back: $t=\ln x$.

$\frac{dy}{dx}=f\!\left(\frac yx\right)$ with $y=ux$

F5 ⇒ $x\frac{du}{dx}=f(u)-u$ ⇒ separable (S1). May need $\sinh^{-1}$, then square.

S6
Behaviour for large $x$uses F1

When: Question says "for large $x$", "as $x\to\infty$", "approximate solution", or "what happens to $y$".

  1. CF terms with $e^{-kx}$ ($k>0$) → 0 as $x\to\infty$. Say so.
  2. Approximate solution for large $x$: $y\approx$ PI.
PI is $\lambda\sin x+\mu\cos x$: give $R\sin(x\pm\varphi)$

A Level $R$-formula: $R=\sqrt{\lambda^2+\mu^2}$; signs of $R\cos\varphi=\lambda$, $R\sin\varphi=\mu$ pick $\varphi$. Method must be shown.

Approximate $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}$ for large $x$

Differentiate the PI.

Find $x$ where $y=0$

Set particular solution $=0$. Solve, often in $\ln$ form.
