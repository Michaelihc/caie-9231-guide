Skills

S1
3 equations: unique solution or not

When: 3 equations in $x,y,z$ with an unknown constant: "find the values of $a$ for which the system does not have a unique solution", or "show it has a unique solution and interpret geometrically".

  1. Left-side numbers, row by row ⇒ matrix $M$.
  2. Find $\det M$ (FP1). Expand along row/column with most zeros.
  3. No unique solution ⇔ $\det M=0$ ⇒ solve for the constant.
  4. Unique ⇔ $\det M\ne0$ ⇒ "3 planes meet at a single point".
  1. start: no unique solution?$$\begin{aligned}2x+3y+4z&=5\\4x+ky+2z&=-2\\6x+9y+10z&=11\end{aligned}$$
  2. left-side numbers ⇒ determinant $=0$$$\begin{vmatrix}2&3&4\\4&k&2\\6&9&10\end{vmatrix}=0$$
  3. expand along row 1$$2(10k-18)-3(40-12)+4(36-6k)=0$$
  4. multiply out$$20k-36-84+144-24k=0$$
  5. collect$$-4k+24=0$$
  6. solve$$k=6$$
"Show unique for all $a>0$"
  • Factorise $\det M$; show each factor $\ne0$ in the given range.
S2
det = 0: consistent or not, which picture

When: $\det M=0$ case of S1: "show the system is consistent / inconsistent", "interpret geometrically", "find the value for which the system is consistent".

  1. Eliminate one variable twice: (eq 2) − multiple × (eq 1), (eq 3) − multiple × (eq 1).
  2. det = 0 ⇒ the 2 new equations have the same left side up to a multiple (maybe negative). Solve both for the same variable.
  3. Same value ⇒ consistent (infinitely many solutions). Different values (or $0=2$) ⇒ inconsistent.
  4. Any 2 rows of left-side numbers multiples? ⇒ name the picture.
  1. start: $k=6$$$\begin{aligned}2x+3y+4z&=5\\4x+6y+2z&=-2\\6x+9y+10z&=11\end{aligned}$$
  2. eq 2 − 2 × eq 1$$(4x+6y+2z)-2(2x+3y+4z)=-2-2(5)$$
  3. simplify$$-6z=-12\ \Rightarrow\ z=2$$
  4. eq 3 − 3 × eq 1$$(6x+9y+10z)-3(2x+3y+4z)=11-3(5)$$
  5. simplify$$-2z=-4\ \Rightarrow\ z=2$$
  6. same $z$ ⇒ consistent$$z=2\ \text{both}\ \Rightarrow\ \text{consistent}$$
  7. no 2 rows multiples ⇒ picture$$\text{sheaf: 3 planes share 1 line}$$
"Consistent for all values of $a$"
  • Find one solution that does not use $a$: set the variable next to $a$ to 0, solve the rest, check all 3 equations.
Find right-side value for consistent
  • Keep it as a letter through the elimination. Set the two results equal ⇒ solve.
S3
Eigenvalues

When: "Find the eigenvalues of $A$", "show that the eigenvalues of $A$ are $-1$, $1$, $5$", "show that the characteristic equation of $A$ is $\lambda^3-\dots=0$", "state the eigenvalues".

  1. Triangular (zeros all below or all above diagonal) ⇒ eigenvalues = diagonal entries. Done.
  2. Else $\det(A-\lambda I)=0$: take $\lambda$ off each diagonal entry. Expand along row/column with most zeros.
  3. Keep common factors like $(4-\lambda)$ outside; don't multiply out.
  4. Solve. Cubic with no factor showing ⇒ factor theorem (try factors of constant term).
  1. start$$A=\begin{pmatrix}3&2&5\\0&4&0\\5&-2&3\end{pmatrix}$$
  2. take $\lambda$ off the diagonal, determinant $=0$$$\begin{vmatrix}3-\lambda&2&5\\0&4-\lambda&0\\5&-2&3-\lambda\end{vmatrix}=0$$
  3. expand along row 2 (most zeros)$$(4-\lambda)\big((3-\lambda)(3-\lambda)-5\times5\big)=0$$
  4. difference of two squares$$(4-\lambda)(3-\lambda-5)(3-\lambda+5)=0$$
  5. simplify$$(4-\lambda)(-2-\lambda)(8-\lambda)=0$$
  6. eigenvalues$$\lambda=4,\ -2,\ 8$$
"Show char. equation is $\lambda^3-\dots=0$"
  • $\det(A-\lambda I)$ starts $-\lambda^3$ for 3×3. Multiply out, then × $(-1)$ to match.
"Show the eigenvalues are …"
  • Still expand $\det(A-\lambda I)=0$ and solve. Plugging in the given values is not what the mark scheme wants.
Given an eigenvector, find its eigenvalue
  • Work out $A\V e$. Answer = number × $\V e$ ⇒ that number.
S4
Eigenvectors

When: "Find corresponding eigenvectors", or first half of "find $P$ and $D$" (S5).

  1. For each eigenvalue: write $A-\lambda I$.
  2. 3×3: eigenvector = cross product of 2 rows. 2×2: row $(p\ \ q)$ ⇒ $\begin{pmatrix}q\\-p\end{pmatrix}$.
  3. Divide out common factor ⇒ simplest whole numbers.
  1. $\lambda=4$: take 4 off the diagonal$$A-4I=\begin{pmatrix}-1&2&5\\0&0&0\\5&-2&-1\end{pmatrix}$$
  2. row 2 is zero ⇒ rows 1 and 3$$\begin{pmatrix}-1\\2\\5\end{pmatrix}\times\begin{pmatrix}5\\-2\\-1\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}8\\24\\-8\end{pmatrix}$$
  3. divide by 8$$\V e_1=\begin{pmatrix}1\\3\\-1\end{pmatrix}$$
  4. $\lambda=-2$: take $-2$ off the diagonal$$A+2I=\begin{pmatrix}5&2&5\\0&6&0\\5&-2&5\end{pmatrix}$$
  5. rows 1 and 2$$\begin{pmatrix}5\\2\\5\end{pmatrix}\times\begin{pmatrix}0\\6\\0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-30\\0\\30\end{pmatrix}$$
  6. divide by 30$$\V e_2=\begin{pmatrix}-1\\0\\1\end{pmatrix}$$
  7. $\lambda=8$: rows 1, 3 are multiples ⇒ use rows 1, 2$$A-8I=\begin{pmatrix}-5&2&5\\0&-4&0\\5&-2&-5\end{pmatrix}$$
  8. rows 1 and 2$$\begin{pmatrix}-5\\2\\5\end{pmatrix}\times\begin{pmatrix}0\\-4\\0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}20\\0\\20\end{pmatrix}$$
  9. divide by 20$$\V e_3=\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\\1\end{pmatrix}$$
Cross product gives $\V 0$
  • Those 2 rows are multiples (or one is zero). Pick a different pair.
S5
Find $P$ and diagonal $D$

When: "Find a matrix $P$ and a diagonal matrix $D$ such that $A^5=PDP^{-1}$" (or $A^{-1}$, $A^2$, $14A+24I$, …).

  1. Eigenvalues (S3), eigenvectors (S4).
  2. $P$ = eigenvectors as columns.
  3. $D$ = diagonal of $f(\lambda)$, same order: $A^5\to\lambda^5$, $A^{-1}\to\frac1\lambda$, $14A+24I\to14\lambda+24$.
  4. Don't work out $P^{-1}$ unless asked.
  1. from S3, S4: want $A^5=PDP^{-1}$$$\lambda=4,\ -2,\ 8:\quad\begin{pmatrix}1\\3\\-1\end{pmatrix},\ \begin{pmatrix}-1\\0\\1\end{pmatrix},\ \begin{pmatrix}1\\0\\1\end{pmatrix}$$
  2. eigenvectors side by side$$P=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1&1\\3&0&0\\-1&1&1\end{pmatrix}$$
  3. eigenvalues down the diagonal, same order$$\begin{pmatrix}4&0&0\\0&-2&0\\0&0&8\end{pmatrix}$$
  4. $A^5$ ⇒ each to the power 5$$D=\begin{pmatrix}4^5&0&0\\0&(-2)^5&0\\0&0&8^5\end{pmatrix}$$
  5. work out$$D=\begin{pmatrix}1024&0&0\\0&-32&0\\0&0&32768\end{pmatrix}$$
Mark scheme traps
  • Column of zeros in $P$ ⇒ 0 marks for $P$.
  • Order in $D$ must match order of columns in $P$.
Given eigenvalues + eigenvectors, find $A$
  • $A=PDP^{-1}$: build $P$, $D$; find $P^{-1}$ (FP1, or S6 on $P$); multiply out.
  • $A^{-1}$ wanted ⇒ $PD^{-1}P^{-1}$. Given $P^{-1}AP=D$ ⇒ $A=PDP^{-1}$.
S6
Use characteristic equation: inverse, powers

When: "Use the characteristic equation of $A$ to find $A^{-1}$", "… to find $A^3$", "… to show $A^4=pA^2+qI$", "… to show $(A-2I)^3=aA^2+bA+cI$".

  1. Characteristic equation (S3), multiplied out: $\lambda^3+a\lambda^2+b\lambda+c=0$. Triangular ⇒ multiply out $(\lambda-d_1)(\lambda-d_2)(\lambda-d_3)$.
  2. Swap $\lambda\to A$, $c\to cI$: $A^3+aA^2+bA+cI=O$.
  3. Inverse: multiply by $A^{-1}$ ⇒ $A^2+aA+bI+cA^{-1}=O$ ⇒ make $A^{-1}$ subject. Work out $A^2$.
  4. Higher power: make $A^3$ subject; multiply by $A$; replace $A^3$ again.
  1. from S3$$(4-\lambda)(-2-\lambda)(8-\lambda)=0$$
  2. multiply out$$-\lambda^3+10\lambda^2-8\lambda-64=0$$
  3. × $(-1)$$$\lambda^3-10\lambda^2+8\lambda+64=0$$
  4. swap $\lambda\to A$, $64\to64I$$$A^3-10A^2+8A+64I=O$$
  5. multiply by $A^{-1}$$$A^2-10A+8I+64A^{-1}=O$$
  6. make $A^{-1}$ the subject$$A^{-1}=-\tfrac{1}{64}\left(A^2-10A+8I\right)$$
  7. work out $A^2$$$A^2=\begin{pmatrix}34&4&30\\0&16&0\\30&-4&34\end{pmatrix}$$
  8. $A^2-10A+8I$$$\begin{pmatrix}34-30+8&4-20&30-50\\0&16-40+8&0\\30-50&-4+20&34-30+8\end{pmatrix}$$
  9. work out$$\begin{pmatrix}12&-16&-20\\0&-16&0\\-20&16&12\end{pmatrix}$$
  10. sub in, times $-\frac{1}{64}$$$A^{-1}=\frac{1}{64}\begin{pmatrix}-12&16&20\\0&16&0\\20&-16&-12\end{pmatrix}$$
Inverse of $A^2$, or $A^{-2}$ in terms of $A$
  • $(A^2)^{-1}=(A^{-1})^2$: find $A^{-1}$ (step 3), then square that matrix.
  • $A^{-2}$ as $pI+qA$: multiply the swapped equation by $A^{-2}$; replace any $A^{-1}$ using step 3.
$(A-kI)^3$ in terms of $A^2$, $A$, $I$
  • Expand like $(x-k)^3$ ($A$ and $I$ commute): $A^3-3kA^2+3k^2A-k^3I$. Replace $A^3$ from step 2.
S7
Prove eigenvalue of a related matrix

When: "$\lambda$ is an eigenvalue of $A$ with eigenvector $\V e$. Show that $\V e$ is an eigenvector of $A^3$ with eigenvalue $\lambda^3$" (or $A^{-1}$, $AB$, $A+kI$).

  1. Write $A\V e=\lambda\V e$ (and $B\V e=\mu\V e$ if given).
  2. Multiply both sides on the left by the matrix needed ($A$, $A^{-1}$, $B$).
  3. Replace each $A\V e$ by $\lambda\V e$. Numbers move to the front.
  4. End with (matrix)$\,\V e$ = (number)$\,\V e$. State: eigenvector $\V e$, eigenvalue = that number.
  1. start$$A\V e=\lambda\V e$$
  2. multiply on left by $A$$$A(A\V e)=A(\lambda\V e)$$
  3. number to the front$$A^2\V e=\lambda(A\V e)$$
  4. replace $A\V e$ by $\lambda\V e$$$A^2\V e=\lambda(\lambda\V e)$$
  5. tidy$$A^2\V e=\lambda^2\V e$$
  6. multiply on left by $A$ again$$A(A^2\V e)=A(\lambda^2\V e)$$
  7. number to the front$$A^3\V e=\lambda^2(A\V e)$$
  8. replace $A\V e$ by $\lambda\V e$$$A^3\V e=\lambda^2(\lambda\V e)$$
  9. tidy ⇒ eigenvalue $\lambda^3$$$A^3\V e=\lambda^3\V e$$
$A^{-1}$
  • Multiply by $A^{-1}$: $\V e=\lambda A^{-1}\V e$. $A$ non-singular ⇒ $\lambda\ne0$ ⇒ divide: $A^{-1}\V e=\frac1\lambda\V e$.
