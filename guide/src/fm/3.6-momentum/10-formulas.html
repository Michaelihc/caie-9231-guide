Formulas

F1
Newton's experimental law (NEL)
$$v_B-v_A=e\,(u_A-u_B),\qquad 0\le e\le1$$

Same + direction for all 4 velocities; $A$ = sphere on the − side (behind). $e=1$ perfectly elastic: no KE lost. $e=0$ inelastic: move on together.

Speed of separation = $e$ × speed of approach.

Approach = how fast the gap closes before. Separation = how fast it opens after.

Learn it: experimental law, no derivation.

F2
Sphere hits fixed surface square on
$$\text{rebound speed}=e\times\text{approach speed}$$

Direction reverses. Signed: $v=-e\,u$.

F1 with the wall as a sphere that never moves: wall velocity 0 before and after.

F3
Oblique impact, two smooth spheres
$$\perp\text{ line of centres: each sphere keeps its own component}$$
$$\text{along: } m_Au_A\cos\alpha+m_Bu_B\cos\beta=m_Ax+m_By,\qquad y-x=e\,(u_A\cos\alpha-u_B\cos\beta)$$

Line of centres = line through both centres at impact. $\alpha,\beta$ = angles of the velocities with it, $x,y$ = along-components after, all signed with + from $A$ to $B$: $B$ heading towards $A$ ⇒ $u_B\cos\beta$ term negative. $B$ at rest ⇒ $B$ moves off along line of centres.

Split each velocity: along line of centres + across it.

Across parts: same after, sphere by sphere. Along parts: S1 (momentum + NEL) on the along parts only.

Picture: impulse only pushes along line of centres, so nothing across changes.

  1. Smooth spheres ⇒ contact force ⊥ both surfaces ⇒ along line of centres. No force across.
  2. No impulse across ⇒ momentum across of each sphere unchanged ⇒ its across velocity unchanged.
  3. Along: impulses equal and opposite ⇒ total momentum kept; NEL holds along the common normal.
F4
Oblique impact with smooth fixed surface
$$v\cos\beta=u\cos\alpha,\qquad v\sin\beta=e\,u\sin\alpha\;\Rightarrow\;\tan\beta=e\tan\alpha$$

$\alpha,\beta$ = angles with the surface (not the normal), before and after. Speed after $v=u\sqrt{\cos^2\alpha+e^2\sin^2\alpha}$.

Along surface: kept. Into surface: × $e$, reversed.

Angle to surface after: tan(after) = $e$ × tan(before) ⇒ comes off flatter (unless $e=1$).

  1. Smooth ⇒ impulse ⊥ surface only ⇒ along-surface component $u\cos\alpha$ kept.
  2. Normal part: F2 ⇒ $e\,u\sin\alpha$ out.
  3. Divide second by first ⇒ $\tan\beta=e\tan\alpha$.
F5
KE loss in an impact
$$\text{KE loss}=\textstyle\sum\tfrac12m(\text{speed before})^2-\sum\tfrac12m(\text{speed after})^2$$

Masses in every term. Oblique: speed² = along² + across²; across parts are unchanged, so they cancel: only along-line-of-centres parts change KE.

KE lost = total KE before − total KE after.

$e=1$ ⇒ none lost. $e\lt 1$ ⇒ some lost.
